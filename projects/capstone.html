Imitation Learning for Long-Horizon Manipulation

The platform is a 16-degree-of-freedom system: two 7-DOF arms plus a 2-DOF neck joint I designed and built myself to give the cameras an active view of the workspace. I collected teleoperation demonstrations and ran both training and inference across all 16 degrees of freedom together, rather than treating the neck as a fixed camera mount.

A dual-arm robot picks up an item, scans its barcode, and sets it down. The task only counts as finished if several stages happen in the right order, and that turned out to be where the difficulty lives. A Diffusion Policy trained on demonstrations learned to scan correctly, then went back and scanned again, and kept doing so indefinitely.

The camera views on the way to the scanner and on the way back are nearly identical, so the policy had no way to tell which half of the task it was in. I'm now conditioning the policy on an explicit stage signal, so that states which look the same but call for opposite actions can be told apart.
